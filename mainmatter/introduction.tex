\chapter{Introduction}
\label{chapter:introduction}


\begin{itemize}
    \item Reason/Relevance: why are your writing this report? (also includes background information)
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Opening statement (to spike interest)
        \item Context, background information (with sources)
        \item Motive (problem or knowledge gap)
        \begin{itemize}
            \item What is the problem in society or science that research could help to solve?
            \item What has already been researched to find a solution for this problem?
            \item What do we not know yet, to be able to solve this problem? (Also called the motivation, the missing piece or the ‘scientific gap’.)
        \end{itemize}
    \end{itemize}
    \item Aim: what are you trying to achieve in your report? Also: method and restrictions, how are you going to achieve it and what is not part of your report?
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Aim, problem statement or research question in the first sentence of the paragraph. "The aim of [text genre] is to [goal] [audience] about/on [a specific topic]."
        \item Method including conditions and restrictions.
    \end{itemize}
    \item Structure: what can the reader expect in your report? (and how are the chapters connected?)
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Overview of the rest of the report.
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
